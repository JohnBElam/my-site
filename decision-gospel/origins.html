Decision Gospel / The Book of Origins How the Scripture Came to Be Written

The Book of Origins

Not the history. The myth. The history names what happened. The myth names what it meant.

There was a meeting.

There are always meetings. This one was unremarkable in every visible way. The slides were prepared. The room was correct. The people with the authority to act on the work were present, which is rarer than it should be, and which felt, at the time, like a good sign.

The work had been done correctly.

The objective had been specified by the people in the room. The metrics had been agreed upon by the people in the room. The expected outcomes had been negotiated and written down and signed off, eleven months before delivery, by the people in the room.

The system was deployed. The lift was real and large and undeniable.

And then the room asked questions the brief had already answered.

Not because the people in the room were incapable. Because the connection between the original asking and the delivered answer had not survived the eleven months between them. The asking had been made by people under pressure making reasonable commitments, and the commitments had been honored, and the people who made them had moved on to other pressures and other commitments, and what remained was the answer sitting in front of people who no longer held the question that had produced it.

The system had done what the system had been asked to do.

The room did not recognize it.

That was the wound. Not a failure of the work. Not a failure of the people. The failure of a structure that did not exist.

Something that would have held the original question in place across the distance between the asking and the answering. That would have kept the decision legible to the people responsible for acting on it. That would have given the answer somewhere to land when it arrived.

The Sealed Common Sense

The common sense of this had been written down.

Not by the person who stood in that room. By someone who had understood the structure underneath the problem and had dedicated years of careful work to naming it, proving it, and writing it with the kind of precision the field demands. The grammar of sequential decisions. The named structure of the state, the transition, the policy, the exogenous information that arrives and changes everything. The spine of the discipline, rigorous and complete.

It was sealed.

Not by intention. By the language it was written in, which was the language of the academy, built for the seminar room, precise in the way that precision requires and inaccessible in the way that precision costs. The people who needed it most would not read it. Not because they were unwilling. Because the door was not built for them.

The common sense existed. The common sense was unreachable. That was the second wound. Different from the first. The first was organizational -- a structure that did not hold the question in place. The second was linguistic -- a language that did not carry the answer to the people who needed it.

Both wounds came from the same source. The gap. The distance between the people who understood how decisions should be engineered and the people who were making the decisions that needed to be engineered.

The Door

Someone showed the door.

Not loudly. Not in a single revelatory conversation. Over time, with patience, in the order the mechanics actually needed to be understood, correcting the early mistakes before they became permanent. The teaching was real and the debt is acknowledged here, at the founding, before anything else.

Because a person who carries the doctrine into new rooms can only carry what they were first taught carefully.

What arrived from that teaching was not just the framework. It was the recognition that the framework was obvious once you could see it. That the structure underneath the problem was not complicated, only sealed. That any reasonable person, shown the bones of the thing, would recognize them -- would feel the click of a well-formed problem meeting a well-formed structure -- and would ask why nobody had said it plainly before.

That recognition changed everything.

Because if the framework was obvious once you could see it, then the failure to see it was a story. The organizations getting it wrong were characters. The gap between the discipline and the rooms that needed it was not a knowledge problem. It was a carrying problem. The doctrine existed. The carrying vehicle was wrong.

The Novel

So they wrote a novel.

Not a paper. Not a methodology deck. Not a certification program. A novel, with characters and a plot and a protagonist who does not understand the discipline at the start and does by the end. Because the people who needed the doctrine were not going to read a paper. Because the distance between the sealed common sense and the rooms where decisions were being made badly required a vehicle that could travel that distance without losing what it was carrying.

The novel worked.

It reached people the journal articles never would. It reached the operations leaders and the product managers and the data scientists who had been doing the wrong thing for years and recognized themselves in the characters. It reached someone who had spent years building the discipline's formal mechanics and understood, from reading a work of fiction, that what had been built could cross the gap.

That crossing was the proof of concept. The carrying was possible. The doctrine could travel if the vehicle was right.

The Gospel

And then, because the novel was not enough -- because there were rooms the novel could not enter, registers the novel would not permit itself to use, people who would not pick up a business book but might find their way to something stranger -- the gospel was written.

This was not the plan.

It emerged from the accumulated frustration of watching organizations spend millions solving the wrong problem with the right tools. From the specific meeting and its specific wound. From the recognition that the sober scholarship existed and was excellent and was sitting in journals unread by the people who needed it most. From the debt to the person who showed the door. From the obligation to carry what had been learned through that door into rooms that would never find the door themselves.

The form was chosen because it was the oldest carrying vehicle the world has ever produced. Because it reaches people that papers and textbooks and certification programs never will. Because it permitted a register the sober version would never permit itself to use -- the commandments, the heresies, the congregation, the grief -- a register that could carry the doctrine sideways into rooms that would have closed their doors to a methodology deck.

The gospel is not sober scholarship.

The church's argument is that the discipline already has enough of that, and that the people who need it most are not reading it, and that sometimes the only way to carry an idea across a gap the serious version cannot cross is to write it in a form the serious version would never permit itself to use.

Whether it works is not for the church to say.

The church is too close to the work to judge the work.

That judgment belongs to the congregation, which is still forming, which is still finding its way to these pages through whatever chain of conversations and recommendations and accidental discoveries led each reader here.

The scripture was not planned.

It emerged from the accumulated frustration of watching organizations

spend millions solving the wrong problem with the right tools.


Read it as doctrine, as satire, or as both.

The distinction matters less than you think.
